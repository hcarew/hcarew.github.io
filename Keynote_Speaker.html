Requirements Engineering Meets Physical AI: Lessons from Human-Autonomy Teaming

Abstract

Generative AI and autonomous agents are rapidly becoming part of Requirements Engineering (RE) practice, supporting activities such as elicitation, analysis, prioritization, and decision-making. As these systems assume increasingly active roles, RE faces challenges that extend beyond automation, including questions of trust, oversight, accountability, and the appropriate division of responsibility between humans and AI.

This keynote examines these challenges through the lens of Physical AI and Human-Autonomy Teaming. Drawing on lessons from the DroneResponse project, a multi-operator, multi-UAV platform developed to study coordination, situational awareness, and supervisory control in human-autonomy teams, it explores parallels between operators supervising autonomous physical systems and requirements engineers collaborating with AI assistants and intelligent agents.

Across both settings, effective outcomes depend not only on the capabilities of AI systems, but also on maintaining calibrated trust, shared mental models, and meaningful human oversight.

The keynote explores how these principles help ensure that AI augments rather than diminishes human expertise while supporting effective collaboration between human stakeholders and autonomous partners.

Speaker Biography

Dr. Jane Cleland-Huang is the Frank M. Freimann Professor and Chair of the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Notre Dame. She received her Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Illinois at Chicago and also holds an honorary doctorate from the University of Gothenburg.

Her research focuses on Requirements Engineering, Human–Machine Interaction, Autonomous Systems, and sUAS Safety Assurance. She directs the Software and Requirements Engineering Research Laboratory, where she leads the internationally recognized DroneResponse project, including a spin-off initiative supporting emergency response and disaster relief.

Dr. Cleland-Huang has served as Program Chair for numerous leading software engineering conferences, including the IEEE Requirements Engineering Conference, ESEC/FSE, ICSE, CAIN, and SPLC. She has also served as Associate Editor for IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering and on several international editorial boards.

Her research has received seven ACM SIGSOFT Distinguished Paper Awards, the Mannfred Paul Award for Excellence in Software Theory and Practice, and two CHI Honorary Mentions. Before joining academia, she worked in refugee camps in South Asia, an experience that shaped her commitment to developing technologies that promote human dignity, inclusion, and societal impact.
